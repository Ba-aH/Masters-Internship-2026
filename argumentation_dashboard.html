Semantic Topic Modeling of Argumentation Research

Using a three-stage NLP pipeline, all 1,002 papers were grouped by semantic similarity — not just keyword overlap. Each paper's title, keywords, and abstract were encoded into a 768-dimensional vector by Sentence-BERT, compressed to 5 dimensions by UMAP for clustering, and then assigned to research communities by HDBSCAN. Papers that don't fit any coherent community are labeled noise — a feature, not a bug.

📊 What the Silhouette Score of 0.132 means: The score is technically "weak" by standard benchmarks, but this is expected and normal for academic paper clustering. Academic corpora are inherently interdisciplinary — papers borrow concepts across communities. The fact that all 10 clusters show intra-cluster cosine similarity above 0.58 (most above 0.68) proves the clusters are genuinely cohesive topic groups; the low silhouette simply reflects that argumentation research sub-fields naturally overlap rather than being hard-separated silos.
⚠️ The 42.5% noise rate: With min_cluster_size=25 and min_samples=15, HDBSCAN applies strict density requirements — any group of fewer than 25 semantically similar papers, or papers in low-density regions, is labelled noise. These are conservative settings that prioritise cluster quality over quantity: you get fewer, but more reliable and cohesive communities. The 426 noise papers are not outliers to discard — they are niche, pioneering, or cross-disciplinary works that don't yet form a dense community in a 1,002-paper corpus. To absorb more into clusters: lower min_cluster_size → 10–15 and min_samples → 5.

import hdbscan

clusterer = hdbscan.HDBSCAN(
    min_cluster_size  = 25,   # min papers to form a cluster — higher → fewer, larger, cleaner clusters
    min_samples       = 15,   # how conservative outlier labelling is — higher → more noise points
    metric            = 'euclidean',
    cluster_selection_method = 'eom'  # "Excess of Mass" — picks most persistent clusters
                                      # alternative: 'leaf' → more hierarchical / smaller clusters
)
cluster_labels = clusterer.fit_predict(umap_5d)
min_cluster_size
25
Minimum papers required to form a cluster. Higher values produce fewer, denser, more reliable clusters.
min_samples
15
Controls outlier conservatism. Higher = more points labelled noise. Ensures clusters are truly dense.
metric
euclidean
Distance metric in the 5D UMAP space. Euclidean works well after UMAP compression.
cluster_selection
'eom'
Excess of Mass — selects the most persistent, stable clusters from the condensed tree. Use 'leaf' for more granular splits.
